# Title

Constraint Satisfaction as a Model for Lexical Reconstruction

---

# Context

During the corpus investigation of the lexical family *w-r-th* in the application of the QREADINGS methodology to Qur'an 15:23, it became apparent that lexical reconstruction did not proceed by selecting an English equivalent or by assigning a dictionary meaning to the lexical family.

Instead, the investigation naturally shifted toward identifying the constraints imposed by the discourse and testing candidate conceptual operations against those constraints.

This observation emerged independently of any conclusion regarding the lexical family itself.

---

# Observation

The discourse appears to generate a set of analytical constraints that any proposed conceptual operation must satisfy.

Lexical reconstruction therefore resembles a process of constraint satisfaction rather than lexical substitution.

The investigation proceeded approximately as follows:

Arabic Text

↓

Grammatical Analysis

↓

Discourse Reconstruction

↓

Conceptual Constraints

↓

Candidate Conceptual Operations

↓

Constraint Testing

↓

Surviving Hypothesis

↓

Conceptual Reconstruction

↓

English Reconstruction

At no stage was a lexical item assigned an English meaning directly.

Instead, candidate conceptual operations were progressively eliminated according to how well they satisfied the constraints generated by the discourse.

---

# Illustrative Example

For Qur'an 15:23, the lexical family *w-r-th* occupies the predicate position of the concluding nominal clause.

Before proposing any conceptual operation, the discourse itself imposed several observable constraints.

Any candidate operation must:

- cohere with the preceding discourse;
- function as an enduring characteristic rather than a momentary action;
- contribute information not already expressed by the preceding verbal processes;
- remain grammatically compatible with the construction;
- remain conceptually coherent within the discourse.

Candidate operations such as:

- remaining;
- succeeding;
- receiving what another leaves;
- continuing after cessation;

were therefore treated as hypotheses to be tested rather than meanings to be adopted.

---

# Distinction from Lexical Substitution

Traditional lexical investigation often follows the sequence:

Word

↓

Meaning

↓

Interpretation

The observed QREADINGS workflow instead appeared to follow:

Discourse

↓

Constraints

↓

Candidate Conceptual Operations

↓

Constraint Testing

↓

Conceptual Reconstruction

↓

Translation

The lexical family contributes a conceptual operation only insofar as that operation satisfies the constraints generated by the reconstructed discourse.

---

# Relationship to Previous Observations

This observation builds upon earlier methodological observations recorded during the development of the Reconstruction Engine.

Specifically:

- corpus investigation is question-driven;
- organised evidence precedes hypothesis generation;
- recurring patterns emerge from organised evidence.

Constraint satisfaction appears to describe the mechanism by which competing conceptual hypotheses are evaluated.

---

# Competing Hypotheses

## Hypothesis A

Constraint satisfaction is simply another description of ordinary hypothesis evaluation.

No modification to the Reconstruction Engine is required.

---

## Hypothesis B

Constraint satisfaction represents the underlying analytical mechanism by which lexical reconstruction operates.

Hypothesis evaluation should therefore be understood as a structured process of testing candidate conceptual operations against discourse-generated constraints.

---

# Current Decision

No change shall be made to the QREADINGS Specification or the Reconstruction Engine.

This observation shall remain under investigation until similar behaviour has been observed across multiple lexical families and independent case studies.

---

# Future Validation

Future investigations should monitor whether lexical reconstruction consistently proceeds by:

- reconstructing the discourse before proposing lexical operations;
- identifying the constraints imposed by grammar and discourse;
- generating multiple competing conceptual operations;
- eliminating candidates that fail one or more constraints;
- retaining only those hypotheses that satisfy the complete set of observable constraints.

If this pattern consistently recurs, consideration should be given to recognising constraint satisfaction as the principal explanatory mechanism underlying lexical reconstruction within the QREADINGS methodology.

---

# Notes

This observation does not propose that lexical families lack conceptual content.

Rather, it proposes that their conceptual contribution is identified through the degree to which candidate operations satisfy the constraints generated by grammar, discourse, and recurring Qur'anic usage.

Accordingly, lexical reconstruction appears to be an inferential process governed by evidence-based constraint satisfaction rather than direct lexical substitution.

Whether this constitutes a defining computational principle of the QREADINGS Reconstruction Engine remains an open methodological question requiring continued investigation.
