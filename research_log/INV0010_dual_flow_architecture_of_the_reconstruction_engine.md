# Title

The Reconstruction Engine May Operate Through Dual Analytical Flows

---

# Motivation

During investigation of Qur'an 90:11-13 (فك رقبة), an apparent contradiction emerged.

The Specification currently states that higher analytical levels constrain lower analytical levels.

However, the investigation itself required morphology, grammar, clause analysis, and construction analysis before discourse structure could be identified.

This suggests that two different forms of dependency are operating simultaneously.

---

# Observation

Two distinct analytical processes appear to occur during reconstruction.

## Flow 1

Structural discovery.

The investigator moves from observable linguistic structure toward discourse.

Morphology

↓

Grammar

↓

Clause Boundaries

↓

Construction

↓

Speech Acts

↓

Discourse

This flow discovers the architecture of the passage.

---

## Flow 2

Interpretive reconstruction.

Once discourse has been reconstructed, explanatory constraints propagate downward.

Discourse

↓

Speech Acts

↓

Construction

↓

Conceptual Participants

↓

Lexical Families

↓

Roots

This flow progressively reduces the available hypothesis space.

---

# Distinction

These two flows perform different functions.

The first establishes structural description.

The second constrains conceptual reconstruction.

Failure to distinguish these processes creates the appearance that the methodology contradicts itself.

---

# Supporting Evidence

Throughout the present investigation:

- discourse could not be identified before grammatical analysis;
- lexical hypotheses never altered previously established discourse analysis;
- higher analytical conclusions consistently constrained lower-level interpretation;
- no convincing case was observed in which lexical reconstruction forced revision of an established discourse analysis.

---

# Working Hypothesis

The Reconstruction Engine is not a single analytical hierarchy.

Instead, it consists of two complementary processes:

1. Bottom-up analytical construction.
2. Top-down interpretive constraint propagation.

The hierarchy therefore describes explanatory authority rather than chronological discovery.

---

# Predictions

If the hypothesis is correct:

- future investigations should consistently require structural analysis before discourse reconstruction;
- once discourse has been securely established, subsequent lexical discoveries should enrich rather than overturn it;
- lower analytical levels should rarely, if ever, invalidate securely established higher-level conclusions.

Any repeated counterexample would weaken the hypothesis.

---

# Relationship to the Specification

No immediate change is recommended.

The current hierarchy may remain valid while its operational behaviour is investigated further.

Future validation should determine whether the Specification should explicitly distinguish analytical discovery from explanatory constraint propagation.

---

# Current Status

Single case study.

Requires validation across unrelated discourse types before consideration for inclusion in the Specification.
