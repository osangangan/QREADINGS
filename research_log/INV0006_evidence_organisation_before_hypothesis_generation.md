# Investigation

INV0006_evidence_organisation_before_hypothesis_generation

**Date:** 3 July 2026

**Status:** Observation

---

# Title

Evidence Organisation as a Prerequisite for Hypothesis Generation

---

# Context

During the application of the QREADINGS Reconstruction Engine to Qur'an 15:23, it became apparent that raw corpus observations were insufficient for generating or evaluating conceptual hypotheses.

Although the current Reconstruction Engine proceeds directly from:

Corpus Investigation

↓

Hypothesis Generation

the investigation naturally required an intermediate activity in which the collected observations were organised into a structured body of evidence.

Only after this organisation did meaningful hypothesis generation become possible.

---

# Observation

Corpus Investigation produces observations.

Hypothesis Generation requires organised evidence.

The transition from one to the other is not automatic.

The investigation naturally proceeded through the following sequence:

1. Corpus Investigation
2. Collection of observations
3. Organisation of observations into a structured evidence table
4. Identification of recurring patterns
5. Hypothesis Generation

The organisation of evidence appeared analytically distinct from both Corpus Investigation and Hypothesis Generation.

---

# Example

Rather than immediately proposing a conceptual operation for the lexical family *w-r-th*, the investigation first sought to organise each occurrence according to common analytical features.

A representative evidence table might include:

- reference;
- discourse context;
- participants;
- observable transition;
- what remains constant;
- recurring conceptual relationships;
- unresolved observations.

Only after the evidence had been organised could competing hypotheses be evaluated against the complete corpus.

---

# Competing Hypotheses

## Hypothesis A

Evidence organisation is already an implicit component of Corpus Investigation.

No modification to the Reconstruction Engine is required.

---

## Hypothesis B

Evidence organisation constitutes a distinct analytical activity.

Corpus Investigation gathers observations.

Evidence Organisation structures those observations.

Hypothesis Generation explains the organised evidence.

---

# Current Decision

No change shall be made to the QREADINGS Specification or the Reconstruction Engine.

This observation shall remain under investigation until similar behaviour has been observed across multiple independent case studies.

---

# Future Validation

Future investigations should monitor whether they naturally perform an evidence organisation step prior to generating hypotheses.

Particular attention should be given to whether investigators consistently:

- organise observations before interpretation;
- compare recurring patterns across the corpus;
- distinguish raw observations from explanatory models;
- delay hypothesis generation until the available evidence has been synthesised.

If this behaviour consistently recurs, consideration should be given to recognising Evidence Organisation (or Evidence Synthesis) as an explicit component of the Reconstruction Engine.

---

# Notes

This observation reinforces a broader methodological principle emerging within QREADINGS:

Observations are not explanations.

Evidence is not a hypothesis.

A hypothesis should emerge from organised evidence rather than directly from isolated corpus observations.

Accordingly, the reconstruction process appears to progress naturally through three distinct analytical activities:

Corpus Investigation

↓

Evidence Organisation

↓

Hypothesis Generation

Whether these represent separate stages or subdivisions of a single stage remains an open methodological question requiring further investigation.
