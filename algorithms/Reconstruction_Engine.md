# QREADINGS Reconstruction Engine

**Version 0.1 (Draft)**

---

# 0. Purpose

The QREADINGS Reconstruction Engine defines the execution model of the QREADINGS methodology.

Where the Specification defines *what* the methodology is, the Reconstruction Engine defines *how* an investigation proceeds.

The Engine specifies the behaviour of the reconstruction process from Qur'anic Arabic text to English reconstruction.

It does not prescribe interpretation. It prescribes execution.

---

# 1. Execution Principles

The Reconstruction Engine operates according to the following principles.

## 1.1 Sequential Execution

Every investigation proceeds through a defined sequence of analytical stages.

The output of each stage becomes the primary input of the next.

---

## 1.2 Iterative Execution

Execution is not strictly linear.

Evidence discovered during later stages may require the investigation to return to an earlier stage.

Reconstruction therefore proceeds through controlled iteration rather than a single forward pass.

---

## 1.3 Explicit State

Every stage shall explicitly record:

* completed outputs;
* unresolved uncertainty;
* rejected hypotheses;
* supporting evidence.

No analytical decision shall remain implicit.

---

## 1.4 Traceability

Every reconstruction shall be traceable to the analytical decisions that produced it.

Every analytical decision shall identify the evidence upon which it depends.

---

## 1.5 Controlled Advancement

A stage shall not be considered complete until its validation conditions have been satisfied.

If validation fails, execution returns to the appropriate earlier stage.

---

# 2. Engine Input

The only required input recognised by the Reconstruction Engine is the Qur'anic Arabic text under investigation.

External resources may assist investigation but do not constitute primary input.

---

# 3. Engine Output

Every investigation produces four outputs.

## 3.1 Primary Reconstruction

The current best reconstruction supported by the available evidence.

---

## 3.2 Residual Uncertainty

Questions that remain unresolved at the conclusion of the investigation.

---

## 3.3 Supporting Evidence

The evidence supporting the adopted reconstruction.

---

## 3.4 Rejected Hypotheses

Alternative reconstructions considered and the reasons for their rejection.

---

# 4. Execution States

At any point, an investigation occupies one of the following execution states.

## Running

The investigation is actively progressing through the reconstruction pipeline.

---

## Backtracking

Later evidence requires re-evaluation of one or more earlier stages.

---

## Waiting for Evidence

The investigation cannot proceed until additional evidence has been gathered or evaluated.

---

## Suspended

The investigation has been intentionally paused.

---

## Completed

No surviving reconstruction better explains the available evidence.

Completion does not imply certainty.

Completion indicates that the investigation has reached its current best explanatory model.

---

# 5. Reconstruction Pipeline

Every investigation proceeds through the following execution stages.

1. Morphological Analysis
2. Grammatical Parsing
3. Constituency Analysis
4. Clause Boundary Detection
5. Speech Act Identification
6. Discourse Structure Mapping
7. Conceptual Relation Reconstruction
8. Corpus Investigation
9. Hypothesis Generation
10. Hypothesis Evaluation
11. English Reconstruction

Each stage is formally specified using the same structure.

* Purpose
* Inputs
* Operation
* Outputs
* Validation
* Failure Conditions
* Next Stage

---

# 6. Validation Rules

Every stage shall satisfy the following requirements before execution advances.

* Every output shall be reproducible.
* Every analytical decision shall be traceable.
* Every hypothesis shall be supported by explicit evidence.
* Every unresolved uncertainty shall be recorded.
* Every rejected hypothesis shall identify the reason for rejection.

---

# 7. Failure Conditions

Execution shall return to an earlier stage whenever:

* competing grammatical analyses remain unresolved;
* competing discourse structures remain unresolved;
* insufficient evidence exists to evaluate competing hypotheses;
* later evidence contradicts an earlier reconstruction.

Failure is not an error.

Failure indicates that reconstruction cannot currently proceed without further analysis.

---

# 8. Completion Criteria

An investigation is complete when:

* every stage has satisfied its validation requirements;
* no surviving hypothesis better explains the available evidence;
* the English reconstruction accurately reflects the current conceptual reconstruction.

Every completed investigation remains open to future revision should new evidence emerge.
